Gravity is nonlinear

$g_{ab} = g^{\rm Kerr}_{ab} + h^{(1)}_{ab} +$ $h^{(2)}_{ab}$ $+$ $h^{(3)}_{ab}$ $+ \dots$
$\mathcal{T}\big[h^{(2)}\big] = \mathcal{S}\big[h^{(1)}, h^{(1)}\big]$
Nonlinearities generate higher harmonics:
$\omega = \omega_{\ell_1 m_1 n_1} + \omega_{\ell_2 m_2 n_2}$

$A_{220\times220} = \mathcal{R}\, A_{220}^2$

Conclusions

  • Black hole spectroscopy can test the nature of black holes
  • Data is picking up: expect dramatic improvements in 10–20 years
  • Will GR pass the test? The devil lies in the next decimal place
  • Nonlinearities, nonmodal effects and data systematics are the challenges ahead
