We do not know when a linear perturbation of Kerr starts to describe the ringdown of a binary black hole merger. We answer this with Lazarus evolutions: evolve the binary with numerical relativity up to a switch time $t_{\rm switch}$, then continue with black hole perturbation theory, starting from $\Psi_4$ and $\partial_t\Psi_4$ read off the numerical data. The perturbative waveform agrees with the full numerical one only once the remnant is close enough to Kerr. The earliest $t_{\rm switch}$ at which they agree tells us when ringdown starts. The same evolutions give the quasinormal-mode amplitudes from first principles rather than from fits, isolate the "prompt" part of the signal through the Green's function, and test whether second-order perturbation theory moves the start earlier.

The hard part is the switch. We build Kerr-like coordinates and a Kinnersley-type tetrad from curvature invariants and the Kerr Killing spinor, rather than from the numerical gauge. The residuals of that same construction measure, point by point, how far the data are from Kerr, so they tell us where the perturbation is actually small.
